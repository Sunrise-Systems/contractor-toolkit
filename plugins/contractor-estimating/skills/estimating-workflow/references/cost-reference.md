# Construction Cost Reference Data

Cost per square foot reference data for construction estimating. **Customize for your market.** The values below are national-average templates — replace them with your own historical data and local market rates.

> **Customization checklist:**
> 1. Pick your market multiplier from the table below (or compute your own from RS Means City Cost Index)
> 2. Apply the multiplier to each $/SF range, or replace ranges entirely with your historical project data
> 3. Add or remove building types based on the work you actually do
> 4. Update annually or after major market shifts (material shocks, labor changes, regulatory updates)

## Escalation Rates

Use these as starting points; check current ENR Construction Cost Index for your market.

| Period | Annual Rate | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| 2024 | 4.0 - 5.5% | Post-pandemic stabilization |
| 2025 | 3.5 - 5.0% | Continued material normalization |
| 2026 | 3.0 - 5.0% | Projected |
| 2027+ | 2.5 - 4.5% | Long-term projection |

**Regional shock premiums** — apply on top of standard escalation when applicable:
- Wildfire / hurricane rebuild zones: +2-5% for trades competing with rebuild labor demand
- Massive data center / fab buildout: +1-3% on MEP trades regionally
- Tariff-affected materials (steel, aluminum, electrical components): +1-3% on those line items

### How to Apply Escalation

Calculate from the estimate date to the **midpoint** of construction:

```
Escalation = Annual Rate × (Months from estimate to construction midpoint ÷ 12)
```

## Customization Notes

**The biggest value of this file is your own historical data.** Pull cost-per-SF actuals from your last 10-20 closed projects, group them by building type, and overwrite the templates above. Within two years of operation, your numbers will be more accurate for your market than any published benchmark.

**Track these metrics per closed project:**
1. Total construction cost ÷ gross SF
2. Cost-per-SF by CSI division
3. Markup percentages actually realized (GC, OH, profit)
4. Contingency consumed vs. budgeted
5. Schedule duration actual vs. estimated

Feed these back into this file annually.
