## Voice Archetypes (pick one during `/initialize`)

Each archetype is a "kind of person" the brand sounds like. The archetype calibrates everything: word choice, sentence rhythm, level of warmth, level of directness, what gets emphasized.

### Option A — The Approachable Expert

**The vibe:** Someone who has built thousands of structures, knows every code and shortcut, and still talks to you like a neighbor over the fence. Not above you. Not dumbing it down. Just straight, clear, and genuinely helpful.

**Core tension:** We know more than almost anyone in the room, but we never make you feel stupid for not knowing it yourself.

**Sample lines:**
- "There was a lot of buzz around it, but I was skeptical it could be more effective than old-school building methods. Turns out I was wrong."
- "The difference between a multi-year nightmare and getting back in your home ASAP will come down to getting on someone's schedule as soon as possible."
- "It might cost a little more up front, but it is — and I can't emphasize this enough — well worth it in the long run."

**Best for:** Family-owned firms, design-build firms, contractors with deep generational relationships in their market.

---

### Option B — The No-Nonsense Builder

**The vibe:** The foreman who's been on job sites since they were 19, has seen every version of every problem, and will tell you the truth even when it costs them the job. No charm offensive. No pitch. Just facts and follow-through.

**Core tension:** We won't sell you. We'll tell you the truth and let the work speak.

**Sample lines:**
- "Here's what it actually costs. Here's why. If you want it cheaper, here's what we'd cut."
- "Most GCs won't give you a real number before contracts. We will. It's not a magic trick — it's just the work."
- "We don't do change orders for things we should have caught. If we missed it, we eat it."

**Best for:** Trade-heavy contractors (electrical, mechanical, civil), GCs serving repeat industrial/institutional clients, firms whose differentiator is delivery discipline.

---

### Option C — The Visionary Advisor

**The vibe:** Thoughtfully direct and warmly strategic. Calm, attentive, operating from abundance — never acting out of urgency or fear. Provides guidance while fostering a bold vision of what's possible.

**Core tension:** We are deeply practical AND we believe what we build should mean something.

**Sample lines:**
- "Most construction companies wait too long to invest in strategic positioning. By the time they realize their backlog is built on three relationships that could disappear, it's already happening."
- "Let's walk through this together. I'm seeing where the disconnect is happening — we can tighten this up in no time."
- "Imagine walking into every meeting knowing your positioning is sharper than anyone else in the room."

**Best for:** Consultative GCs, design-build firms competing on strategy, firms serving sophisticated developer and institutional clients.

---

### Option D — The Premium Craftsman

**The vibe:** Quiet confidence of someone whose work speaks for itself. Doesn't oversell, doesn't undersell. Treats materials, methods, and details as the language of the brand. Believes restraint is a form of respect.

**Core tension:** We could talk for hours about why this joinery matters — but we'd rather just show you.

**Sample lines:**
- "We selected quarter-sawn white oak for the millwork because it's dimensionally stable across the temperature swings this building will see."
- "There's a way to do this that meets code, and there's a way to do this that lasts. We do the second one."
- "The detail you're looking at took the team three iterations. The final one is the simplest."

**Best for:** High-end custom residential, specialty fabrication, restoration contractors, firms whose buyers come from architectural and design backgrounds.

## Tone Attributes (always)

Whichever archetype is chosen, the voice should always feel:

**Conversational** — Write like you talk. Use contractions. Use "you" and "we." Start sentences with "And" or "But" when it feels natural. No stiff corporate speak.

**Confident** — We know our stuff and we say it directly. No hedging with "we think" or "we believe" when stating facts about our expertise. We own what we know.

**Candid** — We tell the truth even when it's uncomfortable. We explain costs, timelines, and trade-offs honestly. This is the foundation of trust. We don't sugarcoat, but we also don't alarm.

**Specific** — Numbers, names, dates, dollar ranges. Specificity is what separates contractor copy from every other contractor's copy.

## Three Tonal Lanes

Within the chosen archetype, modulate tone by context.

| Tone | When to Use | Feel | Example |
|------|------------|------|---------|
| **Helpful** | Working directly with current/future clients | Direct, practical | "Let's walk through this together. I'm seeing where the disconnect is happening." |
| **Effective** | Speaking about {{COMPANY_NAME}}, non-promotional | Confident, proven | "Most builders wait too long to invest in this. By the time they need it, they've already lost." |
| **Encouraging** | Showing what's possible with {{COMPANY_NAME}} | Aspirational, forward | "Imagine walking into final inspection knowing every detail is right." |

## Traits

**Be this:** Empathetic, Understanding, Collaborative, Assured, Grounded, Precise, Consultative, Insightful, Driven.

**Not this:** Sentimental, Indulgent, Patronizing, Cocky, Rigid, Pedantic, Prescriptive, Abstract, Impatient.

---

## Language Rules

1. **Start with clarity.** If it's not clear, nothing else matters.
2. **Ground every claim.** Real numbers, real scenarios, not superlatives.
3. **Control the flow.** One idea per sentence, logical progression.
4. **Keep it clean.** No jargon, no filler, no buzzwords.
5. **Differentiate.** Say what only {{COMPANY_NAME}} can say.
6. **Make it provable.** Avoid claims without evidence.
7. **Know your audience.** Pragmatist? Dreamer? Professional?
8. **Use concrete words.** "3 long-tenured inspectors" not "strategic relationships."
9. **Explain the why.** Don't just state what we do — explain why it matters.
10. **Show, don't claim.** "Permits in 6 weeks" not "We're fast."

---

## Banned Words

Never use:

- synergy / synergistic
- leverage / utilize (say "use")
- holistic
- cutting-edge / state-of-the-art (say what the technology actually is)
- best-in-class / world-class
- innovative
- game-changing
- disruptive / disruption (we're builders, not Silicon Valley)
- scalable (without specific context)
- end-to-end (prefer specifics)
- solutions (vague)
- stakeholders (say "clients," "partners," "team members")
- going forward / at this time / at the end of the day (filler)
- trusted partner (earned by behavior)
- turn-key (without explanation)
- excited to announce (just announce it)

## Copy Don'ts

- No passive voice.
- No emojis in professional content.
- No exclamation marks in body copy.
- No urgency language: "Act now!", "Don't miss out!", "Limited time!"
- No unsubstantiated superlatives.
- No "we" sentences three in a row — vary the subject.
- No corporate fluff openers: "In today's fast-paced construction industry…"

## Writing Formulas

**Headlines:** [Specific benefit] + [proof or specificity]
- "Permits in 6 weeks, not 6 months."
- "One team from first sketch to open sign."
- "{{YEARS_EXPERIENCE}} of building {{MARKET_CITY}}. We know every code, every shortcut, every inspector."

**Opening sentences:** Start with the reader's problem or desire, not with {{COMPANY_NAME}}.
- Bad: "{{COMPANY_NAME}} is a leading construction firm…"
- Good: "Your lease started three months ago and you're still waiting on permits."

**Mixed-audience formula:** [Practical benefit] + [Specific proof] + [Bigger picture connector]
> "We get permits approved in 6 weeks — not the 12 months most people expect. Because our architects, engineers, and builders work under one roof, nothing falls through the cracks. It's how we've done it for {{YEARS_EXPERIENCE}}, and it's why the buildings we build are still standing strong."

**CTAs:** Direct and specific, not generic.
- Bad: "Contact us today"
- Good: "Get a real estimate in 48 hours"
- Good: "Talk to a builder, not a salesperson"

## Sample Calibration: Same Idea, Four Archetypes

The same proof point ("our team is integrated, so plans don't get lost in translation"), rendered four ways:

**Approachable Expert:**
> "Most firms outsource their engineering. That means your architect is sending plans to an engineer they've never met, who sends them back with questions, which go to a GC with a different interpretation. By the time the plans reach the city, they've been through three games of telephone. Our architects and engineers sit ten feet apart."

**No-Nonsense Builder:**
> "Our architects and engineers are on staff. Not contracted, not affiliated — on payroll. When the structural engineer has a question, they walk down the hall. That's it. That's the whole pitch."

**Visionary Advisor:**
> "The cost of fragmentation in construction is invisible until it isn't. A miscommunication between architect and engineer becomes a change order, becomes a delay, becomes a relationship. We integrated those disciplines because the alternative was watching projects bleed value at every handoff."

**Premium Craftsman:**
> "Drawings should be a conversation, not a relay race. Our architects and engineers work in the same room. The details you see in the final set are the result of dozens of those conversations — none of them on email."

## Voice Review — How to Sanity-Check Your Draft

Read every draft against these five questions:

1. **Would the {{VOICE_ARCHETYPE}} actually say this out loud?** If it's phrasing only a marketing department writes, rewrite it.
2. **Could a competitor's name be swapped in without changing the meaning?** If yes, it's not specific enough.
3. **Is every claim backed by a number, a name, or a concrete example?**
4. **Have I told the reader the truth — including the parts that cost us?**
5. **What's the next step the reader should take?** Is it clear?
